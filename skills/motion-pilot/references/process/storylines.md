# Storylines: three films, in words, before anything is built

Most weak films are good motion around no story: a list of features, three cards in a row, a logo, a URL. Read this before any piece over about 15 seconds that tells something: a brand film, a launch, a demo, an explainer, a reel. Skip it for an edit to an existing piece, a single-idea piece (one logo reveal, one kinetic line, a preloader) where the request is the story, or when the client sent a script or storyboard of their own.

## 1. The one thing

- **What it is**, in one line. "You can now…" for a launch; the one idea the viewer must understand for an explainer; "the studio that…" for a brand film.
- **What the viewer remembers** afterwards, in their words, not the slogan.
- **One viewer doing one real task**, or one real situation, from the moment of need to the result. That is the spine. One real thing (a listing, a receipt, a sentence, a number) travels through it from the hook to the payoff.
- Facts and choices stay apart. Facts (what it does, real numbers, real labels) come from `facts.md`. Choices (angle, tone, look) are yours. What the source does not say stays unsaid; an example number is marked illustrative.

## 2. Write three, recommend one

Sketch five or more ideas first, one line each, each pairing a **device** with a **form**. Drop those the subject cannot carry. Of the rest keep the three that differ most, and make one of them the bold one you would not have picked first. Three storylines are three different films, each with its own world, its own device and its own form.

For each, write:

- its name, and its device and form in a few words;
- in two sentences, what the viewer sees and feels;
- the first two seconds, exactly;
- its beats in one line (`hook → … → end card`);
- its look in one line: ground, type, the one material or treatment, taken from the brand or the subject;
- its sound in one line: the bed's feel and tempo, the one moment the music changes, which actions get effects, or silence and why;
- the assets it needs and its main risk.

Put the recommendation first with its reason in one clause. Save all three in `STORYLINES.md` so a later turn can switch. Picking a storyline picks its look and its sound.

- **The client is there to answer:** show the three and stop until they pick. A mix ("A's opening with C's ending") is one storyline.
- **They asked for no stops:** build the recommendation and name the other two in one line each at delivery.

## Devices: what the story is

| Device | What the viewer sees |
| --- | --- |
| A moment in someone's day | A person, a time, the message that creates the need |
| A character does the work | A cursor, a hand or a mascot carries, draws, drags and reacts; every cut has a cause |
| The feature as the hook | The thing working on something recognisable in the first half second |
| The result first | The finished output plays, then the camera pulls back to where it came from |
| One object across scenes | One card, file or listing travels from world to world and is changed by each |
| Before and after | The old way as a scene (vendors in a cloud of chat bubbles), then the new way, calm |
| Type carries the argument | Giant kinetic type and colour fields that change on the beat |
| A number lands | One real number earned by the scene before it, then made physical |
| A question with a blank | "What if your ⟨brand⟩ was the one they copy?", the blank filled by the real thing |
| The mark builds itself | The logo's parts are the story: strokes, guides, panes, a cursor that becomes a nib |
| The object is the explanation | A machine or model whose working is the idea; the camera tours it |

## Forms: how the camera carries it

| Form | What the viewer sees |
| --- | --- |
| One continuous take | No cuts; the camera pushes, pulls and travels from one world into the next |
| Object-to-object chain | Each shot ends on the thing that opens the next: a button becomes a card becomes a phone |
| An endless zoom | The camera dives into a detail that turns out to be the next world |
| A flight across one world | A desk, a map or a board the camera crosses; each idea is a stop |
| Cut on the beat | Hard cuts on the music; type and colour fields change every half beat |
| Match cuts | One shape holds while everything around it changes: a ring becomes a lens becomes the logo |
| Split to one | Several panes play at once, then collapse into the one that matters |
| One frame that rebuilds | A single composition keeps rearranging itself and never leaves the frame |
| Bookends | The closing shot rhymes with the opening (the same motif, resolved), so the film feels whole and loops cleanly |

A pitch where all three storylines are plain cuts between scenes has not tried the forms.

## 3. The hook

The first two seconds are the densest of the film, and frame 0 is the thumbnail: never black, never the logo alone. Open on the thing working, a real moment that creates the need, a question with the input inside it, or a sentence whose last word becomes the picture. Let the old way fail on screen before the brand brings relief. A prompt box being typed into, alone, as the very first shot is the tell of a generated film; as a story step a few seconds in, it is fine.

## 4. Beats

A common shape for a launch or brand film of about 30 to 40 s. Scale it; let the device bend it; a genre file may give its own.

| Beat | Share | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | ~8% | The world and the need, or the thing already working |
| Tension | ~12% | The old way failing on screen, or a joke |
| Reveal | ~8% | The brand or idea named, about 20 to 30% in |
| Proof | ~40% | The real task in the real product, as three to six verbs, one each |
| Payoff | ~20% | The biggest visual moment, once, 60 to 80% in, at full speed |
| Claim | ~8% | The one thing, in one line |
| End card | ~8% | The mark carried in, the name and the action land and hold 2 to 3 s |

## 5. Worlds

A film visits several worlds, each with its own ground, light and scale: the product on its brand colour, a paper desk, a white void with one object, a full-bleed photograph. Enter each world through an object already on screen. Keep one home stage to return to, one carrier the eye follows, and the palette and type constant across worlds. Changing the ground every one or two beats (dark, light, brand colour) is what makes a short film feel produced rather than presented.

## 6. Copy

Titles of at most six words a line, one line on screen at a time, no trailing full stops. Read the lines alone: a stranger gets the pitch. Watch the pictures alone: they tell the same story. With a voice, the screen never repeats the spoken sentence word for word. More in `craft/copywriting.md`.

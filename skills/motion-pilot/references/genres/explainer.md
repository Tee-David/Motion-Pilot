# Explainers

One idea the viewer understands by the end. The spine is one concrete example worked through from start to finish, not a tour of facts. Usually 30 to 90 s.

## Ground it first

Start where the viewer already is: a person, a place or a situation they know, and the question they would ask about it. The abstract device (a diagram, a model, a flow) enters inside that situation as the thing that answers the question, and the film returns to the person at the end with the answer. A film that opens on the diagram alone is a diagram, not an explainer.

## Devices that explain

- **The object is the explanation.** Build a thing whose working is the idea: a machine, a model, a map, drawn so it reads as what it is. The camera tours it: cutaways, an exploded view, a dive into the part that matters.
- **One diagram that grows.** Start from the simplest true picture and add one part per beat, each appearing when it is named. Never a finished diagram dropped on screen.
- **Continuous transformation.** Each step morphs out of the last (a bar becomes an axis, a dot becomes a population) so the thread is never cut.
- **The worked example.** One real input (a sentence, an invoice, a photo) travels through every step to the answer.

## Shapes of information

Most lines are one of a few shapes, each with its own picture: a number that lands; a myth struck through and the truth printed over it; before and after; many things converging into a few; a network that densifies; one chosen out of many; steps in order; a real screenshot with the detail circled; a quote set as a poster. Pick the shape first, then the picture, so similar lines get different pictures.

## Words

- Narrated: the voice is the clock. Reveal each part when the voice names it, across the back half of the scene; keep a scene to about 19 spoken words (about 9 s).
- Not narrated: large lines carry it, up to about 10 words, each held 0.5 s plus 0.3 s a word.
- Labels sit on the part they name and arrive with it. No legends, no bullet slides.

## Getting it right

Every claim, value and step traces to `facts.md`. Check the logic of each step before animating it: a beautiful wrong diagram is worse than none. End on the one idea in one line; a brand end card only when asked.

## Tells

Bullets; icons in circles; three cards; the whole diagram at once; a narrator reading the text on screen word for word.
